# Waypoint surface scout

[Open the four-page concept book](Waypoint-Surface-Scout-Concept-Book.pdf).

This is the current Waypoint design: one small humanoid robot with a smooth oval head, two recessed camera lights, a standard removable power pack, and a shared tool mount. All units use the same body and optics. Roles come from the tool they carry and the work they do.

The 36/33/31 brief carries forward as Bot Crossing's 3D movement and readable poses, Wobbly Life's playful follow-through, and the Mars Mission figure's quiet readiness. The clay texture is the rendering style. In the story, the body has ceramic-like protective panels over covered joints.

The turnaround is the chassis reference. Use its smooth head shell for any model; the field illustration is a staging and mood reference. The Moon and Mars scenes show a robot carried to the surface by a lander and operating from a service dock. This is concept art, with no claims of flight qualification or working hardware.

## Illustration prompts

Both published illustrations were generated with the built-in image tool.

### Moon and Mars field scenes

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset: wide 3:2 horizontal cinematic illustrated diptych for WAYPOINT, original small humanoid robotic lunar and Martian field scouts. Two equally wide adjacent scenes with a crisp central cut, no text. Left half is Moon: vacuum black sky, hard white sunlight, charcoal gray regolith and sharp shadow. A compact Waypoint scout has just stepped off a small uncrewed lander ramp onto the dust, one low boot finding its balance and helmet slightly catching up, next to low utility cargo and a visible cargo rail. Right half is Mars: rusty soil, muted dusty peach sky, soft reddish daylight. An identical scout crouches by a simple waist-high beacon it is placing alongside a compact modular habitat, one hand turning an oversized service latch. Each robot entirely visible, fills 60 percent of its half's height, slightly raised angled camera similar to a 3D colony game. Robot design identical across both scenes: trimmed agile upright body around five and a half head heights tall, ordinary human-shaped oval pressure head with rounded crown and chin, no fin or ear shapes or antenna. Built-in smoked copper capsule-shaped faceplate with two tiny amber recessed optical points under glass, no face or skin. Chalk ivory ceramic body with one diagonal persimmon chest band, black ribbed sealed joints, rounded segmented mechanical gloves, squat petrol teal dust tread boots, petrol teal wedge battery cassette on the back. Distinctively tactile miniature clay stop-motion art, broad toy-like clean shapes with subtle sculpted texture, full environments are hand-made miniature sets. The machines look purposeful, sturdy and quietly funny. No weapons, no flags, no graphics, no words, no logos or watermark.

### Standard chassis turnaround

Use case: stylized-concept. Asset: wide landscape 3:2 single character model turnaround for WAYPOINT, from the supplied lunar/Mars diptych. Input image 1: visual identity reference. Present THREE orthographic full-body views of the SAME compact robot on a warm ivory seamless backdrop: front, left profile, back. Precisely aligned scale and shared baseline, equal spaces, generous margins, clean studio shadows, head and feet visible. Preserve the small generic humanoid chassis from the field diptych, its subtly hand sculpted ivory matte ceramic, persimmon diagonal chest band, graphite bellows under rigid panels, three chunky articulated fingers and thumb, short rugged petrol teal boots and rectangular beveled petrol teal dorsal power cassette with a grab handle. Robot proportions trim but sturdy, head is an ordinary oval human-head silhouette with a crown and subtly tapered chin. Blank side shell: remove any side disks, fins, ear hardware, or protrusions. Front face is a single smoked-copper oval inset with two small warm recessed camera lights. No mouth, no skin, no eyebrows, no humanoid facial anatomy. Shoulders and hips modest, waist straight, little character about 5 to 5.5 heads tall. Hands at relaxed neutral angle so full silhouette is readable and no arms overlap the torso. Show a compact universal tool socket on wrist as part of the chassis. No tools in hand. No props, text, captions, arrows, lines, borders, words, weapons or logos. Treat as premium stop-motion maquette photography, not glossy toy or hard sci-fi.
